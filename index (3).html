Corporate investment account (CCPC / HoldCo)
Annual corporate additions are made while Person A is younger than the stop age above. For example, a stop age of 60 permits additions at ages 55–59 and stops them starting at age 60. Corporate tax treatment is approximate: it models passive-income tax, a combined refundable tax pool, CDA creation on realized gains, and dividend refunds. It does not model every T2 pool or GRIP/ERDTOH distinction.
Recurring passive income (rental / other ordinary income)
Enter the actual annual cash left from the property/business source before personal income tax separately from the taxable income reported on the tax return. They can differ because cash expenses and tax deductions are not always the same. Income is allocated between spouses using the ownership percentage above and is included in personal tax and OAS-clawback calculations. Income is received while Person A is at or above the start age and below the stop age.
Before retirement: any after-tax passive cash is treated as additional to the explicit RRSP/TFSA/non-registered/corporate additions above and is reinvested. If those stated annual additions are actually funded from this passive income, start this income at retirement or reduce the contribution inputs to avoid double-counting.
Spending & withdrawal policy
The one-time purchase is inflation-adjusted from today's dollars and is always funded at 100% if assets permit. It is excluded from the guardrail withdrawal-rate benchmark and is never reduced by the spending guardrail.

What this version fixes

Portfolio depletion risk is tracked separately from spending cuts: a path is counted as depleted if gross investable assets fall to $1,000 or less before the final planning year. Taxes are part of the cash-flow equation: withdrawals continue until the household has enough after-tax cash to meet the spending target. Non-registered sales use FMV and ACB to calculate realized gains. RRSP→RRIF conversion age is explicit; mandatory RRIF withdrawals begin in the year after conversion, and surplus cash is reinvested rather than disappearing. One-time purchases are separated from recurring spending: they are funded in full, excluded from the guardrail benchmark, and never reduced by a guardrail cut. They can still affect later years by lowering the remaining portfolio. Recurring passive income is now modelled separately as cash flow and taxable ordinary income, with spouse ownership, start/stop ages and optional inflation indexing.

2026 personal-tax basis

The annual model starts with 2026 federal and British Columbia brackets, 2026 basic personal amounts, dividend gross-ups/credits, 50% capital-gains inclusion, and OAS recovery tax. Federal brackets are projected with simulated inflation. B.C. brackets are held at 2026 levels through 2030 to reflect the announced B.C. indexation pause, then indexed thereafter.

Investment returns

Monte Carlo mode uses FP Canada's 2026 geometric assumptions, converted to arithmetic means with correlated shocks. In v2.6+, the Cholesky shock is applied once at its full covariance-scaled volatility (correcting the double volatility scaling in v2.5). v2.8 also reports the best/worst one-year and rolling three-year compounded personal-portfolio returns actually encountered in each run. Historical / Hybrid mode aligns Person A's retirement age to each start year and uses a long-run U.S. total-equity-return proxy and U.S. government-bond total-return proxy, with Canadian CPI inflation, through 2025. When a tested retirement extends beyond 2025, the missing tail switches to the same seeded Monte Carlo engine. Historical mode is therefore a sequence-risk stress test, not an exact reconstruction of a Canada/U.S./international portfolio.

Non-registered accounts

Equity distributions are approximated as one-third of expected equity return, consistent with FP Canada's discussion that 33% of equity return is a reasonable dividend assumption. Canadian-equity dividends receive eligible-dividend treatment; foreign dividends and fixed-income distributions are treated as ordinary income. Capital appreciation remains unrealized until sale.

Corporate account

The HoldCo model is intentionally labelled an approximation. It separately models passive-income tax on distributions, refundable tax, CDA creation from the non-taxable half of realized capital gains, and dividend refunds. It does not reproduce all CCPC tax pools or the exact treatment of every security. Use an accountant's projections for implementation decisions.

Sources built into this version

CRA 2026 federal/BC rates: Canada.ca current-year tax rates
BC rates/credits: B.C. personal tax rates
FP Canada: 2026 Projection Assumption Guidelines
TFSA room: CRA TFSA room
Historical return proxies: Jordà-Schularick-Taylor Macrohistory Database (pre-1928) and Aswath Damodaran U.S. stock/bond annual returns (1928–2025)
Canadian inflation: long-run Canadian CPI plus Statistics Canada annual-average CPI for 2021–2025.

Important omissions

No GIS, no AMT, no foreign tax credits, no charitable credits, no disability credits, no detailed age/pension credits, no principal residence, no RRSP contribution-room calculation, RRIF minimums use the account holder's age (not an elected younger-spouse age), no first-death/second-death rollover engine, and no full T2 corporate-tax calculation. These omissions are deliberate so the model remains transparent.
