What this version fixes

v3.4: adds an automatic five-allocation retirement-outcome comparison using identical underlying paths and reports full/flexible spending success, purchasing-power failure, severe shortfall, depletion, market drawdown, spending cuts, years below target, estate values, and early-sequence resilience. v3.3: improves the Ending After-Tax Liquidation Value chart with multiple rounded X-axis dollar labels and vertical guides so intermediate ending values are easier to estimate. v3.2: fixes a v3.1 dashboard rendering error caused by a missing decision-status function. v3.1: replaces the old hard-clamped loss floor with a smooth Monte Carlo tail boundary. Extreme randomized returns are compressed as they approach the selected loss limit rather than being forced to exactly the same number. Historical proxy years remain unaltered. Return diagnostics now emphasize a bad-decile path statistic and separately report the absolute worst observation and raw Monte Carlo draws that crossed the selected boundary. v2.9: adds a user-adjustable maximum one-year blended portfolio drop in Advanced settings and improves Safari/iPad interaction with standard event listeners, larger touch targets, and no automatic 10,000-path run on page load. Portfolio depletion risk is tracked separately from spending cuts: a path is counted as depleted if gross investable assets fall to $1,000 or less before the final planning year. Taxes are part of the cash-flow equation: withdrawals continue until the household has enough after-tax cash to meet the spending target. Non-registered sales use FMV and ACB to calculate realized gains. RRSP→RRIF conversion age is explicit; mandatory RRIF withdrawals begin in the year after conversion, and surplus cash is reinvested rather than disappearing. One-time purchases are separated from recurring spending: they are funded in full, excluded from the guardrail benchmark, and never reduced by a guardrail cut. They can still affect later years by lowering the remaining portfolio. Recurring passive income is now modelled separately as cash flow and taxable ordinary income, with spouse ownership, start/stop ages and optional inflation indexing.

2026 personal-tax basis

The annual model starts with 2026 federal and British Columbia brackets, 2026 basic personal amounts, dividend gross-ups/credits, 50% capital-gains inclusion, and OAS recovery tax. Federal brackets are projected with simulated inflation. B.C. brackets are held at 2026 levels through 2030 to reflect the announced B.C. indexation pause, then indexed thereafter.

Investment returns

Monte Carlo mode uses FP Canada's 2026 geometric assumptions, converted to arithmetic means with correlated shocks. In v2.6+, the Cholesky shock is applied once at its full covariance-scaled volatility (correcting the double volatility scaling in v2.5). v3.5 uses separate editable downside and upside portfolio-return boundaries. Randomized returns are smoothly compressed only in the extreme tails rather than hard-clipped; the diagnostics report raw draws that crossed either chosen boundary. The headline 1-year diagnostics use bad-decile worst-year and good-decile best-year statistics, while the absolute extremes remain visible underneath. Historical / Hybrid mode aligns Person A's retirement age to each start year and uses a long-run U.S. total-equity-return proxy and U.S. government-bond total-return proxy, with Canadian CPI inflation, through 2025. Historical proxy years are never altered by the Monte Carlo tail boundaries; only any randomized post-2025 tail is limited. Historical mode is therefore a sequence-risk stress test, not an exact reconstruction of a Canada/U.S./international portfolio.

Non-registered accounts

Equity distributions are approximated as one-third of expected equity return, consistent with FP Canada's discussion that 33% of equity return is a reasonable dividend assumption. Canadian-equity dividends receive eligible-dividend treatment; foreign dividends and fixed-income distributions are treated as ordinary income. Capital appreciation remains unrealized until sale.

Corporate account

The HoldCo model is intentionally labelled an approximation. It separately models passive-income tax on distributions, refundable tax, CDA creation from the non-taxable half of realized capital gains, and dividend refunds. It does not reproduce all CCPC tax pools or the exact treatment of every security. Use an accountant's projections for implementation decisions.

v4.0 focused decision support

v4.0 does not replace the preserved tax, account, withdrawal, guardrail or Monte Carlo equations. It adds paired re-runs of the same engine for allocation recommendation zones, longevity horizons, withdrawal strategies and major-purchase stress. Main-run calculations use the selected simulation count; compact sensitivity screens use up to 1,500 paired paths for responsiveness and disclose that fact in the interface.

Important omissions

No GIS, no AMT, no foreign tax credits, no charitable credits, no disability credits, no detailed age/pension credits, no principal residence, no RRSP contribution-room calculation, RRIF minimums use the account holder's age (not an elected younger-spouse age), no first-death/second-death rollover engine, and no full T2 corporate-tax calculation. These omissions are deliberate so the model remains transparent.

Purchasing-power failure means planned real recurring spending was not fully funded in at least one retirement year while more than $1,000 of investable assets still remained. Bad-decile max market drawdown is the 90th percentile of each path’s maximum peak-to-trough decline in a return-only retirement portfolio index, so withdrawals do not artificially create the drawdown. Early-sequence resilience shows full-spending success among the worst 20% of first-five-retirement-year return sequences. The recommendation zone does not declare a universal winner. It separates a lower-volatility choice, a balanced retirement choice, and a growth/legacy choice. Downside spending protection carries substantially more weight than median terminal wealth.
